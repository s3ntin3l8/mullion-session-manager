Tasks mode and Workspace mode — wireframes, rev 9

Proposal for #2976. Mullion gets two first-level modes: Workspace (the dockview grid, as today) and Tasks (the kanban board, front and center). These are low-fidelity wireframes: they show layout and flow, not final visuals. Each frame is drawn at its real size (desktop 1280, fold 840×900, phone 390) and scaled down. recommended marks the option I would pick; open marks a choice that needs your decision. Open the tappable fold prototype to try the open questions (stacking, two rails) at real size on the device.

4 · Phone: where the mode switch goes decided: B

A bottom bar is the natural place for a two-way switch on a phone, but in Workspace mode the bottom edge belongs to the terminal key bar. Three ways to resolve it. All frames show Workspace mode with a terminal, the case where they differ; in Tasks mode there is no key bar, so every option can use the bottom bar of frame 3a.

6 · Where task detail lives: three options decided: A

The earlier task redesign deliberately merged every task detail into the right sidebar, to remove redundancy. Sections 1 to 3 above (option B here) split it again: a panel in Tasks mode and a glance in the sidebar. These are the alternatives, drawn on the fold where it matters most. Top row: Tasks mode with a task open. Bottom row: the same task reached from Workspace mode.

  • Why A. It keeps the single task-detail surface the earlier redesign built, it keeps the sidebar identical in both modes, and it is the smallest change: on desktop, board plus sidebar detail is what exists today. The reported problems came from the drawer floating and from the stale selection, not from the detail living in the sidebar.
  • What A gives up. The detail is a sidebar-width column (resizable, but not a wide panel), and it replaces the Git and GitHub views while shown instead of sitting beside them; the existing task ↔ PR links cover that hop. On the fold, board plus docked sidebar leaves room for about two lanes.
  • Phone is the same in all three: no sidebar there, so the detail is a full screen.

7 · The sidebar on the fold: is there something better than docking? decided: 1

On desktop the sidebar is always docked. The fold has 840px, so the question is what an open sidebar does to the terminal in Workspace mode. Three ways, none of them the old dim-and-dismiss drawer.

  • Why 1. One behaviour on desktop and fold, the terminal and the task visible together, and no layer that can be dismissed by accident. Its cost is that the terminal reflows each time the sidebar opens or closes, exactly as it does on desktop when you toggle the pinned sidebar.
  • A rule for narrower tablets. Docking needs room. Where the workspace would be left with less than about 400px (windows narrower than roughly 800px), the sidebar opens full width as in 3 instead. So there are two behaviours in total, docked or full width, and floating is gone everywhere.
  • In Tasks mode there is no terminal to reflow, so 1 simply shows two lanes beside the task (section 6, option A).

8 · Fold: a rail on each half idea

The mode switch and the session switcher move out of the top bar into a thin left rail. Each half of the fold then has one rail on its outer edge and one pane beside it: the left rail chooses what the left half shows, the right rail what the right half shows. The top bar goes away. These frames are the live prototype, so they can be tapped here too.

  • What it buys. Both rails sit under the thumbs when the fold is held with two hands; the top bar is the hardest place to reach. The 52px top bar goes, which is about three more terminal rows. The seam lands on the hinge.
  • What it costs. The left pane loses 24px against frame 2c (372 instead of 396), about three terminal columns. A 48px rail cannot name a session, only hint at it, so the full picker stays one tap away.
  • openOne session or the grid. With the sidebar open the left half fits one pane, so the rail switches sessions. With the sidebar closed the grid could come back (rail entries focus a pane), or the fold could always show one session per half.
  • openDesktop. With this, the switch would sit in three places: top bar on desktop, left rail on the fold, bottom bar on the phone. Giving desktop the same left rail brings it back to one pattern (a rail, laid flat on the phone).
  • Against earlier decisions. "Left sidebar hidden in Tasks mode" still holds for the project and session tree; the rail is new, thin chrome that stays. Applies to the tablet tier by width, not to the fold as a device.

9 · Desktop with the same left rail idea

If the fold gets a left rail, desktop takes it too, so the mode switch sits in one place. The rail is thin, global navigation: the mode switch, one chip naming the current workspace, and New, the command box, the bell and settings at the bottom, so there is no top bar at all. The left sidebar stays what it is today, the full workspace tree with projects, sessions and devices, open by default. Collapsed, the rail lists the sessions itself, which is exactly the fold layout.

  • Why the rail does not list the workspaces. With around eleven workspaces in three groups the list would fill the rail, and initials cannot tell Mullion from Management. The names stay in the sidebar, where they are readable. The rail shows only the one you are in.
  • One "New" button. It opens a short menu: session, task, workspace, workspace group. The first entry follows the mode (session in Workspace, task in Tasks). One plus in a 52px rail instead of one per section.
  • Workspace groups stay in the sidebar as they are. When the sidebar is collapsed (and on the fold) the workspace chip opens the same grouped list as a picker, and the chip takes the group's colour.
  • Devices. A device screen is a pane, so an open one sits in the rail beside the sessions. The Devices list (start, stop, open) lives in the panel, and in the "All" picker when the panel is collapsed.
  • Where the top bar's controls go. Board toggle: the mode switch. Sidebar toggle: the panel's "«". New, command box, bell, settings: the rail's bottom cluster. Theme: into settings. The phone keeps its own bar.
  • decidedFold, Workspace mode: sessions on the right half live in the right rail. At most two sessions show, one per half, remembered per device. A long press moves a session to the other rail. In the right rail a session sits above the sidebar views and behaves like those tabs: tap shows it, tap again hides it.
