Tasks mode and Workspace mode — wireframes, rev 18

Proposal for #2976. Mullion gets two first-level modes: Workspace (the dockview grid, as today) and Tasks (the kanban board, front and center). These are low-fidelity wireframes: they show layout and flow, not final visuals. Each frame is drawn at its real size (desktop 1280, fold 840×900, phone 390) and scaled down. recommended marks the option I would pick; open marks a choice that needs your decision.

1 · Desktop approved

No top bar. A thin left rail is the global navigation: the mode switch, a chip naming the current workspace with one colour square per workspace group under it, and the command box, the bell and settings at the bottom. The left sidebar stays what it is today (workspace tree, projects, sessions), open by default, and only in Workspace mode. The right sidebar is the same in both modes and always docked.

  • Why the rail does not list the workspaces. With around eleven workspaces in three groups the list would fill the rail, and initials cannot tell Mullion from Management. The names stay in the sidebar, where they are readable.
  • decidedGroup squares. They show which groups exist and which one you are in. Chip and squares are one target: it opens the workspace list. On the fold that list is a sheet over the left half with rows at touch size (frame 2e).
  • "＋" means "add a session here" and always sits directly under a list of sessions. There is no global New button: a workspace or a group is created in the workspace list, as today, and a task from the board.
  • decidedDevices are a view in the right sidebar on desktop and fold, beside Git and GitHub: the list with start and stop. On the fold the screen shows in the view, on the right half. On desktop a device's screen opens from the list as a pane in the grid. The left sidebar's Devices section goes, so there is one home.
  • Where the top bar's controls go. Board toggle: the mode switch. Sidebar toggle: the sidebar's "«". Command box, bell, settings: the rail's bottom cluster. New session: the "＋" under the sessions. Theme: into settings. The phone keeps its own bar.

2 · Fold (840 × 900, unfolded) approved

A rail on each half: rail 48, pane 372, pane 372, rail 48, so the seam sits on the hinge at 420px. The left rail chooses what the left half shows, the right rail what the right half shows. It is the desktop layout with the left sidebar collapsed. These frames are the live prototype and can be tapped; open it full size on the device.

  • Why rails. Both sit under the thumbs when the fold is held with two hands; a top bar is the hardest place to reach. Dropping the 52px top bar gives about three more terminal rows. The cost is 24px of pane width, about three terminal columns.
  • Which sessions show is remembered per device. The workspace layout shared with desktop is not edited.
  • Moving a session between halves: the button in the pane's header. The "＋" under each rail's sessions means the same on both sides, add a session to this half (new, another existing one, or one from the other half). A long press on a rail entry is a shortcut. Dragging is not offered.
  • No docked left sidebar on the fold. Its contents are reached from the rail: the workspace chip opens the workspace list, "＋" opens the session sheet, and Devices is a right-rail view.
  • decidedOne rule for companions on the fold. Anything you look at beside a session is a right-rail view: Tasks, GitHub, Git, Files, Device, Browser, Dock. The left half is always a session. On desktop Browser and Dock stay as they are (a pane in the grid, the bottom dock).
  • Dock actions on touch. What sits in hover menus and small kebabs today becomes visible buttons for the selected row (restart, stop or start, branch for a command, check for update for a service), and a sheet with 52px rows for the stack-wide ones. The image tag stays on the row and marks an available update. The Dock's GitHub row and browser shortcut are dropped here because GitHub and Browser are rail views of their own.
  • Why the Dock log is read only here. The Dock is switched off under a touch pointer today, on purpose: the key bar sends input by session, and cannot tell a session's own pane from a Dock monitor of the same session. A read-only log avoids that; typing into a monitor means opening it as a session.
  • Checked by the owner on the fold (in the prototype): rail reach, the board switching between lanes and stacked, the long press and the sheets are fine, and two sessions side by side are readable in today's app. Real terminal behaviour, the on-screen keyboard and performance can only be checked in the implementation.
  • Narrow tablets. Where a half would be narrower than about 400px (windows under roughly 800px) an open sidebar view takes the full width instead (section 7).

4 · Phone: where the mode switch goes decided: B

A bottom bar is the natural place for a two-way switch on a phone, but in Workspace mode the bottom edge belongs to the terminal key bar. Three ways to resolve it. 6 · Where task detail lives: three options decided: A

historyDrawn before the top bar was replaced by the left rail (sections 1 and 2). The decision recorded here still holds; the top bar in these frames does not.

The earlier task redesign deliberately merged every task detail into the right sidebar, to remove redundancy. Sections 1 to 3 above (option B here) split it again: a panel in Tasks mode and a glance in the sidebar. These are the alternatives, drawn on the fold where it matters most. Top row: Tasks mode with a task open. Bottom row: the same task reached from Workspace mode.

  • Why A. It keeps the single task-detail surface the earlier redesign built, it keeps the sidebar identical in both modes, and it is the smallest change: on desktop, board plus sidebar detail is what exists today. The reported problems came from the drawer floating and from the stale selection, not from the detail living in the sidebar.
  • What A gives up. The detail is a sidebar-width column (resizable, but not a wide panel), and it replaces the Git and GitHub views while shown instead of sitting beside them; the existing task ↔ PR links cover that hop. On the fold, board plus docked sidebar leaves room for about two lanes.
  • Phone is the same in all three: no sidebar there, so the detail is a full screen.

7 · The sidebar on the fold: is there something better than docking? decided: 1

historyDrawn before the top bar was replaced by the left rail (sections 1 and 2). The decision recorded here still holds; the top bar in these frames does not.

On desktop the sidebar is always docked. The fold has 840px, so the question is what an open sidebar does to the terminal in Workspace mode. Three ways, none of them the old dim-and-dismiss drawer.

  • Why 1. One behaviour on desktop and fold, the terminal and the task visible together, and no layer that can be dismissed by accident. Its cost is that the terminal reflows each time the sidebar opens or closes, exactly as it does on desktop when you toggle the pinned sidebar.
  • A rule for narrower tablets. Docking needs room. Where the workspace would be left with less than about 400px (windows narrower than roughly 800px), the sidebar opens full width as in 3 instead. So there are two behaviours in total, docked or full width, and floating is gone everywhere.
  • In Tasks mode there is no terminal to reflow, so 1 simply shows two lanes beside the task (section 6, option A).

8 · New session: the launcher approved

"＋" opens a launcher that only launches; ⌘K stays the command palette (commands, and launchers by typing). Choosing an agent selects it and shows its options in place: the model and the skip-permissions override. Today those appear only while hovering a row, which does not work on touch and is fiddly with a mouse. Desktop and touch show the same choices in the same order.

  • "Open existing" is touch-only. On desktop, sessions are listed under their projects in the left sidebar, as today, so the desktop launcher has no such tab. On fold and phone the tab is that same list (by project, the project named under each title), since there is no sidebar to show it.
  • Skip permissions keeps today's model: a per-agent setting, shown as the flag on the tile, plus a per-launch override, the switch.
  • One more step than today for an agent: select, then Launch (or Enter, or double click). That step is what makes the model and the override reachable without hovering.
  • Not drawn: a "recent launches" row, and agent arguments beyond the model.
